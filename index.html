READ THE RESULT

A clock-time check.
A better question for your scheduler.

Missing time: the local clock skips over your selected time. No UTC instant has that local date and time.

Repeated time: the local clock passes your time twice. Both UTC candidates are shown.

A scheduler may skip, shift, or deduplicate these cases. This tool finds time ambiguity; it doesn’t predict a particular cron engine’s execution policy. It checks local wall-clock schedules, not fixed elapsed-time intervals.

Results use your browser’s timezone database and its projected rules. Update your browser and verify critical schedules against the runtime you deploy. Known 2026 changes for Vancouver, Edmonton, Inuvik, Winnipeg, Casablanca and El Aaiun are checked; incompatible runtime data blocks affected reports. This limited check does not certify other zones. Rule-change source (checked 19 October 2026).

Using this with n8n or cron

Check the timezone configured in your workflow and instance. In n8n, the workflow timezone takes precedence over the instance timezone. Save and publish your workflow after changes, and verify actual executions around a transition.
